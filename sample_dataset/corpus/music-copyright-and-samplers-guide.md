# Music Copyright and Sample Clearance Guide

## Overview

Music copyright protection safeguards original musical compositions and sound recordings. Sampling—taking a portion of one sound recording and reusing it in a new composition—requires explicit legal authorization from rights holders.

## Dual Copyright System

Under copyright law, every commercial track consists of two distinct protected elements:

1. **Circle-C Copyright (©)**: The Musical Work / Composition (Melody, Chords, Lyrics).
2. **Circle-P Copyright (℗)**: The Phonogram / Sound Recording (The physical or digital master audio file).

## Sample Clearance Workflow

Using even a 1-second sample of an existing commercial track without authorization constitutes copyright infringement. Clearing a sample requires securing permissions from both rights holders:

1. **Master Sample Clearance**: Negotiated with the record label that owns the original recording.
   - Typically requires an upfront clearance fee ($1,000 to $10,000+) plus a master royalty split (e.g., 3% to 15% of new master earnings).
2. **Publishing Sample Clearance**: Negotiated with the music publisher(s) representing original songwriters.
   - Requires granting co-writing credit and a percentage split of the new composition's publishing rights (e.g., 10% to 50%+ based on sample prominence).

```
Step 1: Identify Master Label & Composition Publisher via ISRC/ISWC lookup
Step 2: Submit formal sample request letter with audio demo & timestamp breakdown
Step 3: Negotiate upfront fee + master royalty % with Master Label
Step 4: Negotiate publishing ownership % + songwriter credit with Publisher
Step 5: Execute legal clearance contracts before commercial release distribution
```

## Interpolation vs. Sampling

- **Sampling**: Directly extracting audio samples from the original sound recording. Requires clearing **both** Master (℗) and Composition (©) rights.
- **Interpolation (Re-recording)**: Re-singing or re-playing a musical melody or lyrics from another song without using the original audio recording. Requires clearing **only** the Composition (©) publishing rights, saving master licensing fees.

## AI Generated Music & Copyright Eligibility

As of 2024–2026 guidelines issued by the US Copyright Office and EU Copyright Directive:
- AI-generated audio outputs generated solely via text prompts without human authorship are **ineligible for copyright protection**.
- Human-authored elements (original human lyrics, human-arranged instrumentation, human vocal performances) integrated with AI tools remain eligible for copyright registration, provided human authorship is clearly documented.
