# Record Label Deals and Contracts Overview

## Overview

Record agreements govern the financial and legal relationship between recording artists and record labels (Major Labels vs. Independent Labels). Contracts dictate ownership of master recordings, royalty rates, advances, marketing budgets, and distribution channels.

## Main Types of Label Agreements

### 1. Traditional Major Label Deal
- **Master Ownership**: Label owns master recordings in perpetuity or for a long-term duration (e.g., 20+ years).
- **Advance**: Upfront non-refundable lump sum paid to the artist, which is **recouped** from future artist royalties.
- **Royalty Rate**: Artist receives **15% to 22%** of gross master earnings; label retains 78% to 85%.

### 2. 360 Agreement (Multiple Rights Deal)
In a **360 Deal**, the record label participates in all financial income streams generated by the artist, including:
- Recorded music royalties
- Live touring and performance income (~10% to 20%)
- Merchandise sales (~15% to 30%)
- Commercial sync placements and endorsements (~15% to 25%)
- Publishing income (if co-publishing rights are acquired)

```
                 +--------------------------------------+
                 |          360 Deal Revenue Streams    |
                 +-------------------+------------------+
                                     |
    +-----------------+--------------+---------------+------------------+
    | Recorded Music  | Live Touring | Merchandise   | Sync & Sponsorship|
    | (75%-85% Label) | (10%-20% Label)|(15%-30% Label)| (15%-25% Label) |
    +-----------------+--------------+---------------+------------------+
```

### 3. Profit-Split / Independent Deal
- Common among independent labels (e.g., XL Recordings, Beggars Group, Ninja Tune).
- Master royalties and revenues are split **50/50** after all direct production, distribution, and marketing expenses are fully recouped.
- Master rights often revert to the artist after a specified term (e.g., 5 to 10 years).

### 4. Distribution & Services Deal (Distro Deal)
- The artist retains **100% master ownership**.
- Distribution companies (e.g., AWAL, Virgin Music Group, Empire) charge a **10% to 15% distribution fee** or flat subscription rate while providing automated distribution, pitching, and accounting tools.

## Recoupment and Financial Mechanics

- **Cross-Collateralization**: Allows a label to use earnings from a successful album or single to recoup unrecouped balances from an unsuccessful prior release under the same agreement.
- **Unrecouped Advances**: Recording advances are non-returnable. If an album fails to generate sufficient royalties to pay back the advance, the artist does not owe cash directly out-of-pocket to the label; however, no royalty checks will be issued until the balance reaches zero.
